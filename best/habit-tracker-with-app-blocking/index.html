Which habit trackers also block distracting apps?

A tracker can record the useful behavior. A blocker can interrupt the competing behavior. The important question is how directly the two are connected.

Updated July 22, 2026

The short answer

There is no single best habit tracker with app blocking because the connection can work in different ways. Ascent connects one goal, a 70-day daily plan, a smaller fallback, reflection, and optional Screen Time friction. Habit Doom locks selected apps and lets completed habits earn screen time. If you want a mature tracker or a deeper blocker, a two-app stack such as Streaks plus one sec or a tracker plus Opal may fit better than either integrated product.

The apps may look similar in a feature checklist while creating very different daily loops.

Choose the architecture, not the longest feature list

Choose Ascent if

  • One meaningful goal needs a guided daily plan.
  • A two-minute fallback matters on low-capacity days.
  • Blocking should protect the replacement action rather than become the entire product.

Choose Habit Doom if

  • You want selected apps locked until habits earn access.
  • Screen time is a motivating reward for you.
  • You prefer a direct transactional connection between completion and access.

Choose a two-app stack if

  • You need many mature habit records.
  • You need the deeper intervention or analytics of a specialist blocker.
  • You do not mind maintaining separate systems.

When Ascent is not the better choice: choose Habit Doom when earning screen time is the mechanism you want. Choose Streaks plus one sec when established habits need polished Apple tracking and the opening reflex needs intervention. Choose a tracker plus Opal when multi-habit records and deep focus rules matter more than a one-goal plan.

A note on ScreenZen

ScreenZen's first-party description includes an app goal alongside delays, short access sessions, schedules, limits, and strict blocks. That is a screen-time goal, not evidence that ScreenZen is a general positive-habit tracker. It can be the entire attention-control layer, but a separate positive-habit system may still be useful.

Frequently asked questions

Which habit tracker blocks distracting apps?

Ascent combines one 70-day positive-habit plan with optional Screen Time friction. Habit Doom documents a different integrated model: selected apps are locked and completed habits earn access time. A traditional tracker can also be paired with a specialist blocker.

Is it better to use one app or a separate habit tracker and blocker?

One integrated app reduces setup and can connect the block to the positive behavior. A two-app stack is better when you need a mature multi-habit tracker or a deeper specialist blocker. The trade-off is maintaining two systems.

How is Habit Doom different from Ascent?

Habit Doom ties completed habits to earned screen time. Ascent ties optional blocking friction to one guided 70-day goal plan with daily actions, a two-minute fallback, widgets, and reflection. The first emphasizes access as a reward; the second emphasizes a planned replacement behavior.
