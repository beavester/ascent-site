Editorial standards

How we research habit and focus apps

A comparison is useful only when you can see where its facts came from, which parts are judgment, and who benefits from the recommendation.

Effective July 23, 2026

The short version

HabitBuilding.xyz is published by the maker of Ascent. We review public product documentation and App Store listings to compare habit and focus apps. Unless a page explicitly says otherwise, these are editorial comparisons, not hands-on product tests.

We aim to describe what each product is built to do, identify the reader it fits, and say plainly when a competitor is the better choice.

Sources we prioritize

Claims are only as trustworthy as the source behind them. We use the closest available first-party source and do not fill gaps with guesses.

For product facts

  1. Current Apple App Store listing
  2. Official product website
  3. Official feature, help, pricing, and privacy documentation
  4. Official company announcements for material changes

For behavioral claims

  1. Peer-reviewed research closest to the claim being made
  2. Government or university-hosted evidence summaries
  3. Books or secondary explanations only when their limits are clear

Documented fact versus editorial judgment

A documented fact

A feature, platform, integration, limit, or policy that appears in a current first-party source. We link the source and avoid extending the claim beyond what it actually says.

An editorial judgment

Our interpretation of fit: for example, whether an app is better for running a known routine or designing a new behavior. We present that as a recommendation, not as a laboratory result.

Important distinction: documented features tell us what a product offers. They do not prove that every person will use it consistently or achieve a particular outcome.

How we compare apps

We compare mechanisms and intended jobs instead of assigning a decorative score.

  1. Primary jobWhat problem is the app mainly designed to solve?
  2. Planning supportDoes it help define the behavior or assume the behavior is already known?
  3. Daily executionWhat happens at the moment the user needs to act?
  4. Attention interventionCan it interrupt competing behavior, or does it operate away from that moment?
  5. FeedbackDoes it emphasize logs, streaks, reflection, a character, coaching, or screen-time data?
  6. Scope and privacyWhich platforms and permissions are documented, and what does the product say about its data practices?

Our relationship to Ascent

HabitBuilding.xyz is published by the maker of Ascent: Habit Builder & Focus. That relationship is disclosed on comparison and decision pages.

Ascent is relevant when someone wants one goal, guided daily actions, a smaller fallback, iPhone visibility, reflection, and optional Screen Time friction in one loop. It is not automatically the answer. A specialist tracker, planner, routine timer, or blocker should be recommended when that narrower job fits the reader better.

Corrections and updates

Product features change. A visible update date means the material claims on that page were reviewed or changed; it is not refreshed merely to look current.

If a source disappears or no longer supports a claim, we replace the source or remove the claim. If a page cannot offer distinct value, we consolidate it rather than pad it.

To request a correction, email hello@habitbuilding.xyz with the page URL and a first-party source that supports the change.

How the app index is maintained

The maintained iOS habit app index stores each app as a structured record with a primary job, best-fit statement, main limitation, platforms, pricing model, capability states, first-party sources, and a verification date.

Capability values are deliberately controlled. “Yes,” “limited,” and “no” summarize what the reviewed documents support. “Not confirmed” means we did not find enough current first-party documentation to make the claim; it does not mean the feature is impossible or absent.

Feature claims can change after publication. We keep the visible date tied to material verification, link the source used for each record, avoid exact volatile pricing, and correct or remove unsupported claims when they are found.

What we do not do

  • We do not have access to anyone's private ChatGPT or search prompts.
  • We do not sell a better editorial ranking.
  • We do not invent hands-on tests, prices, ratings, credentials, or outcomes.
  • We do not present Ascent or another habit app as medical treatment.
  • We do not claim that crawler access guarantees indexing or an answer-engine citation.
